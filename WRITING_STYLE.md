# AI/BI Lakehouse Substack — Writing Style

The voice for the monthly AI/BI Lakehouse newsletter (https://aibilakehouse.substack.com),
co-authored by Robin and Tobi. This is the newsletter's own style, separate from any
personal or account writing style. Edit it freely as the voice evolves; the skill reads
this file as the single source of truth for tone and mechanics.

## Who we are writing for

Data practitioners: analytics engineers, BI developers, data engineers, and the people
who lead them. They are technical, time-poor, and skeptical of marketing. They read us
to learn what changed, what it means for their work, and how to solve a real problem.

## Voice

- **Practitioner to practitioner.** We are peers sharing what we see in the field, not a
  vendor pitching. Knowledgeable, curious, and willing to have an opinion.
- **"We" and "you".** We write as Robin and Tobi ("we"), and we address the reader
  directly ("you"). Keep it personal and conversational, not corporate.
- **Low hype, high signal.** No superlatives stacked on superlatives. If something is a
  big deal, show why with a concrete example, not adjectives.
- **Grounded in reality.** Favor "here is a problem we keep seeing and how to solve it"
  over feature announcements read from a changelog. Releases matter because of what they
  let a practitioner do.

## Content mix

Each issue blends three kinds of material. A good issue has at least two of them, and
does not read as a pure release roundup:

1. **What shipped.** New or notable Databricks AI/BI, Genie, dashboards, or SQL
   capabilities from the prior month, framed by why a practitioner should care.
2. **A thought or perspective.** A short opinion, trend, or lesson. Where the space is
   heading, a pattern worth naming, a myth worth correcting.
3. **A real-world problem and solution.** A concrete question or pain point (often
   surfaced from customer conversations) and a practical way to handle it.

## Structure

- **Hook first.** Open with the single most interesting thing in the issue, or a question
  the reader has probably asked themselves. Earn the scroll.
- **Scannable.** Short subheads, short paragraphs (2 to 4 sentences), and one clear idea
  per section. Bullets only when a list genuinely helps.
- **One visual per Main point.** A screenshot, GIF, or diagram that shows the thing, not a
  decorative image.
- **Close with a takeaway or a small call to action.** What to try, what to watch, or a
  question to reply with.

## Length

- **Issue:** roughly 600 to 1000 words total. Respect the reader's inbox.
- **Main section:** about 150 to 250 words each, 2 to 4 per issue.
- **Extras:** one to three sentences each, with a link.

## Mechanics

- **No em dashes.** Use commas, colons, periods, or parentheses instead.
- **Active voice, present tense** where natural.
- **Plain words over jargon.** Define an acronym the first time. Cut filler ("in order
  to" becomes "to", "leverage" becomes "use").
- **Every factual or metric claim needs a public source** you can link. If you cannot
  link it publicly, do not state it as fact.
- **Links inline** on the noun they describe, not as bare URLs.
- **Numbers and product names exact.** Get feature names and availability (GA, Public
  Preview, Beta) right.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)

- **Public sources only in what we publish.** Every claim, link, screenshot, and example
  must come from a publicly available source. Drop anything gated, internal, or
  unreleased.
- **Anonymize anything drawn from customer conversations.** Slack threads and customer
  questions are inspiration only. Never name a customer, person, or account, never quote
  an internal message, and never include anything that could identify them. Reframe the
  problem generically.

## Quick do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Genie now answers follow-up questions in context. Here is where that helps." | "Revolutionary new Genie capabilities you can't miss!" |
| "We keep seeing teams struggle with X. Here is a cleaner pattern." | Quoting an internal Slack thread or naming the customer who asked. |
| Link the public blog post or release note. | Cite an internal doc, a go/ link, or an unreleased feature. |
| One screenshot that shows the feature working. | A stock image or a screenshot with customer data in it. |
